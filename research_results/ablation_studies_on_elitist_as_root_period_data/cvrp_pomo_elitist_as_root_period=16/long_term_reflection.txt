Simplicity, fixed parameters, and spatial clustering consistently outperform complex demand-aware or adaptive heuristics. Multi-scale proximity (k-NN + global inverse distance) with depot bias forms a robust backbone. Demand awareness is best applied as mild post-hoc penalties—not in clustering or neighbor selection. Problem-size adaptation shows promise but risks overfitting; balanced static configurations generalize better. Future work should focus on lightweight, end-to-end trainable biases integrated into POMO’s architecture to enable route-state awareness without disrupting spatial coherence.
