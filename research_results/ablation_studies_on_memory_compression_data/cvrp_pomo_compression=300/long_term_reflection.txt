The latest experiments reinforce a critical principle: static heuristics—no matter how structurally informed or carefully tuned—hit a hard performance ceiling on large-scale CVRP instances (~146 vs. ideal ~93), confirming that adaptivity through end-to-end differentiability is non-negotiable. While separating depot-customer and customer-customer logic and incorporating demand compatibility improve stability, these gains plateau without learning. The failure of prototype-based gating and multiplicative ensembles underscores that complexity without differentiability harms scalability and gradient flow. Conversely, simplicity with clear inductive biases (e.g., proximity, capacity pressure) provides a stable foundation—but only when embedded within a trainable framework. A key emerging pattern is that global statistics (n, total demand, demand variance) are valuable signals, yet their utility is unlocked solely through lightweight, differentiable mappings rather than fixed rules. Persistent challenges include maintaining solution entropy during training and ensuring heuristic signals remain expressive across scales. This solidifies the research pivot: future work must replace handcrafted heuristics with minimal neural modules inside the decoder that contextually reweight edges using learnable functions of global features. The path forward lies not in refining static formulas, but in co-designing policy and heuristic components that evolve together, guided by interpretable biases but unshackled from rigid, non-adaptive logic.
