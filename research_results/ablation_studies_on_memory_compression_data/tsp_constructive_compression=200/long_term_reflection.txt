Recent experiments confirm that lightweight graph-theoretic proxies—like simplified clustering coefficients based on local distance statistics—effectively capture topological structure without computational overhead, reinforcing the principle that structural awareness matters more than complex lookahead. However, performance still lags on larger and clustered instances, underscoring that local heuristics alone cannot recover global geometric signal absent in raw distance matrices. The consistent failure of adaptive or multi-step mechanisms further validates that minimalism and clarity in phase logic yield better results than added complexity. Crucially, the persistent gap versus top baselines highlights a fundamental limitation: constructive heuristics require either explicit spatial embeddings or proxy representations that infer latent geometry (e.g., via spectral methods or MDS). Future work must prioritize embedding-aware node selection, hybrid regret-destination formulations with dynamic weighting, and phase transitions informed by both local density and global tour progress. Testing across diverse instance types—especially clustered ones—remains essential to ensure robust generalization beyond uniform Euclidean assumptions.
