The latest experiment reinforces that ordinal stability in item rankings is the cornerstone of effective ACO for the Multiple Knapsack Problem. The near-ceiling performance (35.7470) achieved by combining hard feasibility masking, L∞-normalized prize-to-weight ratios, and a *feasible-subset-only* conflict penalty confirms that ranking integrity must be preserved even when incorporating combinatorial awareness. Crucially, this refined conflict penalty—applied only among feasible items and using a smooth exponential downweighting—demonstrates that *localized*, *bounded* adjustments can enhance heuristic discrimination without disrupting the global order that ACO relies on for convergence. This validates the principle that static heuristics are not limited by expressiveness but by their inability to adapt to state-dependent capacity consumption during construction. All attempts to introduce global scaling, alternative norms (e.g., L1), or unbounded penalties degrade performance, especially on larger instances, underscoring that noise or rank inversions—even subtle—are more damaging than simplicity. The persistent gap between current scores (~35.75) and theoretical optima highlights the fundamental entanglement of valuation and assignment: static heuristics assign desirability in isolation, ignoring how early selections constrain later choices. Successful strategies consistently share three traits: (1) exclusion of infeasible items upfront, (2) bottleneck-aware resource modeling via L∞, and (3) perturbations restricted to feasible subsets with minimal rank distortion. Failed approaches violate one or more of these. Moving forward, research must shift from refining static item scores to rearchitecting solution construction itself—either through a two-stage process (static ranking followed by dynamic, capacity-aware placement) or hybrid heuristics that modulate initial ranks with real-time residual capacity feedback while preserving ordinal bias. Additionally, pheromone updates should reward *feasible marginal gains* under actual loads, not just raw prize. The path to breakthroughs lies not in smarter static valuations, but in decoupling selection priority from assignment context within a dynamically informed yet rank-stable framework.
