Recent experiments confirm that lightweight, static heuristics combining bottleneck, geometric mean, and entropy signals—adaptively weighted by constraint tightness (using average slack, not min-slack)—yield robust performance. Inter-constraint coupling via covariance adds marginal gains but increases complexity. Dynamic or learned adaptations consistently underperform due to fragility and overfitting. The optimal balance remains in simple multiplicative blending with λ≈0.1 and blend≈0.65–0.7. Persistent gaps include small/large instance trade-offs and lack of true construction-time adaptivity. Future work should focus on stable ensemble-like static heuristics and efficient approximations of high-value item distribution across constraints.
