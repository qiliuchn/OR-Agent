The latest experiments confirm that high-performance online bin packing hinges on modular, distribution-aware orchestration rather than monolithic heuristic complexity. The discrete threshold-based ensemble—leveraging entropy and coefficient of variation to switch between specialized heuristics—outperforms continuous or simplified alternatives, reinforcing that signal orthogonality and architectural modularity are essential. Crucially, the persistent 0.1% gap between current performance (1.66%) and the theoretical limit (1.55%) stems not from suboptimal blending, but from the priority function’s inability to access early-instance statistics due to statelessness. This validates the core insight: adaptivity must be externalized via resettable, instance-level characterization rather than embedded in reactive bin-state logic. Successful strategies consistently preserve a four-component structure (best-fit base, gap penalties, efficiency bonuses, consolidation signals) with entropy-modulated fragmentation awareness. Future progress requires lightweight, stateless mechanisms to infer distributional priors from initial items—enabling policy selection over heuristic blending—and exploring learning-augmented initialization that encodes distribution knowledge without violating online constraints. The path forward lies in orchestrating simple, pre-optimized heuristics through robust, early-signal-driven selectors, not in refining internal priority computations.
