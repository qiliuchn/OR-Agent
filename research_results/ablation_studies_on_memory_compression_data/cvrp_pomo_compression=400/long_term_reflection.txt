The latest experiment reaffirms and sharpens the central thesis: static heuristics—whether hand-crafted or pseudo-learned via fixed neural modules—are fundamentally misaligned with the sequential, state-dependent nature of CVRP feasibility. The attempted end-to-end trainable bias module, though conceptually aligned with inductive bias learning, failed to improve performance (score 72.95 vs. baseline ~71.47), confirming that even 'learned' static features cannot encode dynamic residual capacity without access to decoding trajectory states. This reinforces three robust principles: (1) raw distance is the most reliable and scalable attention prior; (2) minimal, localized demand penalties (weight ≤0.1, RBF-smoothed) offer consistent marginal gains; and (3) depot-return incentives must be weak to avoid route fragmentation. Critically, all attempts to embed capacity awareness into pairwise edge scores—via geometric proxies, local demand density, or static embeddings—consistently degrade performance, especially on large instances (e.g., 145 vs. ~55 on 1000 nodes), exposing a fundamental representational gap. The recurring U-shaped performance curve—where increasing heuristic complexity first harms, then (upon simplification) recovers, and again harms performance—validates that simplicity and stability are essential for compatibility with POMO’s parallel multi-start framework. These findings decisively shift the research frontier from external heuristic design to internal architectural co-design. Future progress requires lightweight, differentiable modules embedded within the decoder that maintain approximate, per-trajectory residual capacity estimates in a parallelizable manner. Such modules must condition attention biases on both static inputs (distance, demand) and dynamic decoding states, enabling the policy to learn context-sensitive routing intuition through reinforcement learning. Permutation equivariance, parameter efficiency, and seamless integration with POMO’s training dynamics remain non-negotiable. The path forward is not better static rules, but learned, dynamic bias generation fused with the decoder’s evolving representations—bridging the gap between combinatorial structure and sequential decision-making.
