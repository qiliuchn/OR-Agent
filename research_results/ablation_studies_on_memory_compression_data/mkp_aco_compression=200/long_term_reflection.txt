Recent experiments confirm that simplicity, proper normalization, and multiplicative fusion of bottleneck efficiency, total efficiency, and inter-constraint balance yield robust static heuristics for MKP. The coefficient of variation outperforms entropy-based metrics for measuring weight balance, reinforcing that lightweight statistical measures align better with problem structure than complex information-theoretic constructs. Empirical tuning (e.g., bottleneck exponent 1.2) surpasses theoretical defaults, highlighting the value of data-driven calibration. However, the fundamental limitation of static heuristics—lack of responsiveness to residual capacity during ant construction—remains unresolved, creating a persistent performance gap. This underscores the need for hybrid approaches: preserving the stable geometric backbone while introducing minimal online adaptivity, such as real-time modulation of heuristic values based on current knapsack occupancy. Future work should prioritize low-overhead, state-aware adjustments that retain scalability without sacrificing the robustness of the offline design. The path forward lies not in increasing heuristic complexity, but in smartly bridging offline stability with online awareness.
