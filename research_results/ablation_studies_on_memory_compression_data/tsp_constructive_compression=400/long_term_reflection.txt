The latest experiment reinforces and refines a core principle: in constructive TSP heuristics, performance is maximized not by adaptive complexity but by disciplined simplicity with carefully balanced static signals. The failure of adaptive k-selection—despite its intuitive appeal for balancing exploration and exploitation—confirms that introducing even modest dynamic behavior during greedy construction degrades robustness across problem scales. This aligns with prior evidence that fixed, interpretable scoring (specifically the 4-factor system: proximity, diversity, destination awareness, origin incentive) already captures the essential local structure of Euclidean TSP instances. Notably, the optimal configuration remains k=5 with fixed weights (-0.4, +0.3, -0.1, +0.2), suggesting that candidate set size and weight tuning are interdependent hyperparameters best optimized jointly via systematic search rather than heuristic adaptation. The consistent underperformance of context-sensitive strategies implies that greedy node selection is inherently fragile to noise or overfitting, especially as tour length increases. This strengthens the hypothesis that constructive heuristics should act as stable, high-quality initializers—not global optimizers—and that true performance gains will come from coupling them with post-construction refinement (e.g., 2-opt, Lin-Kernighan). Persistent gaps remain in understanding *why* certain static weights work so well and whether alternative diversity metrics (e.g., angular spread, Voronoi cell coverage) could improve spatial balance. Future research should prioritize: (1) exhaustive grid search over small k and weight combinations to identify globally optimal static parameters, (2) replacing average-distance diversity with geometrically informed alternatives, (3) formalizing endgame thresholds (e.g., why ≤8 nodes benefit from exhaustive search), and (4) integrating the current heuristic as a warm-start generator for local search metaheuristics. Critically, all new designs must preserve implementation fidelity through rigorous unit testing and logging to avoid confounding algorithmic ideas with coding artifacts.
