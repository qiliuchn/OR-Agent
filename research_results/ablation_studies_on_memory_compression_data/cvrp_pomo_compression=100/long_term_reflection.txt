Static pairwise heuristics fundamentally fail to capture sequential feasibility in CVRP, especially at scale. Recent experiments confirm that even sophisticated edge-level proxies—like marginal insertion costs or angular coherence—degrade performance due to their inability to model dynamic route states and capacity constraints. Simpler demand compatibility remains relatively robust but still insufficient. This reinforces that effective guidance must be dynamic, sequence-aware, and differentiable, integrated during decoding rather than precomputed. Future work must abandon static attention biases entirely in favor of real-time, route-level feasibility signals that adapt to partial solutions and remaining capacity.
