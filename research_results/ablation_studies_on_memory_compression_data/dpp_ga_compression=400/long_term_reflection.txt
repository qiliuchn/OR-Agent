The latest findings reinforce and refine a central thesis: in the Decap Placement Problem, crossover is a stable diversity engine, but intelligent, constraint-aware repair is the true performance bottleneck and opportunity. Experiment #1’s success—using a spatial clustering metric fused with a geometric prior—confirms that evolutionary signals must encode not just *where* decaps are placed, but *how they co-occur locally*, capturing electromagnetic coupling effects missed by simple frequency counts. This validates the principle that hybrid sensitivity maps (physics + population) outperform single-source guidance, especially when weights are fixed yet tuned to domain intuition (e.g., 0.7/0.3). Crucially, the persistent underperformance on high-constraint instances like Instance 3 underscores that even advanced repair fails without direct access to instance metadata (probe location, forbidden zones). The current architectural separation between crossover and constraint context remains the primary barrier to generalization. Vectorized uniform crossover continues to serve as an efficient, scalable backbone—complex adaptive crossovers add no value—confirming that innovation should focus downstream. Two interdependent upgrades now emerge as critical: (1) pipeline redesign to inject instance-specific constraints into the repair phase, enabling probe-relative or obstacle-aware priors instead of generic center-based ones, and (2) lightweight complexity metrics (e.g., forbidden zone density, probe proximity) to dynamically modulate the balance between geometric and evolutionary signals. Generalizable principles solidify: hard constraints must be respected early, surrogate guidance must be both physically grounded and spatially contextual, and local placement patterns matter more than global statistics. Future work must prioritize embedding contextual intelligence into repair—not evolving crossover mechanics—while ensuring all signals are computed efficiently via vectorization to maintain scalability.
