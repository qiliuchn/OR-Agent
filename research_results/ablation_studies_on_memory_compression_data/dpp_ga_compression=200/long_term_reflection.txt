Recent experiments confirm that geometric awareness is indispensable for effective decap placement, but must be grounded in accurate constraint information. While adaptive grid partitioning and spatial smoothing of parent-derived usage maps improve performance—reaching a score of 13.259—they remain fundamentally limited by the absence of explicit prohibited zone and probe location data. The persistent underperformance on Instance 1 underscores this gap: inferred constraints cannot fully capture complex layout topologies. Hybrid crossover strategies (40-40-20) with alpha blending [0.35, 0.65] and Manhattan-based duplicate resolution remain robust, especially when combined with balanced importance maps (0.3/0.7 base-to-usage split). However, true progress requires shifting from simulated to grounded spatial reasoning. Future work must prioritize lightweight integration of real constraint maps into vectorized crossover logic, enabling instance-adaptive yet scalable operations. This represents the critical path toward resolving structural bottlenecks and achieving consistent high performance across all instances.
