The cumulative evidence from Experiments #1–#5, when integrated with the prior long-term reflection grounded in Experiments #1–#10, further solidifies and refines our understanding of static heuristic design for the Multiple Knapsack Problem under Ant Colony Optimization. The core principles remain robust: (1) **Discrete norm selection among L1, L2, and L∞ efficiency metrics** is consistently superior to any form of blending, adaptive weighting, or learned mapping—whether via linear regressors, softmax interpolation, or conflict-aware prize adjustments; (2) The **fixed-weight discriminative score** (0.45·variance + 0.35·coefficient of variation + 0.20·range) is not only empirically optimal but also theoretically justified as a stable, interpretable selector that avoids overfitting to instance noise; and (3) **Adaptive power transformation** using base ≈2.4 scaled by efficiency variance (clipped to [0.5, 2.0]) remains essential for modulating heuristic sensitivity, while attempts to replace variance with robust statistics (e.g., MAD) or impose exponent ceilings degrade performance, confirming that moderate amplification of signal variance is beneficial.

Crucially, all five recent experiments reinforce a decisive pattern: **any deviation from signal purity—introducing soft combinations, second-order corrections, inter-item proxies, or learned selectors—consistently underperforms relative to the pure discrete selection pipeline**. This confirms that the marginal performance gap between experimental scores (~35.79–35.80) and the parent solution’s 35.816 is not due to missing heuristic sophistication, but rather to implementation fidelity and the inherent stochasticity of ACO execution. The repeated failure of even minimally interactive heuristics (e.g., conflict penalties, residual capacity estimates) underscores a fundamental limitation: **static, item-wise heuristics cannot encode state-dependent desirability, knapsack-specific opportunity costs, or combinatorial complementarity**, which are essential for optimal packing.

This convergence of evidence across diverse experimental designs—norm blending, conflict awareness, learned selectors, two-phase corrections, and robust statistics—collectively validates that we have reached the **practical ceiling of static heuristic engineering** within the current ACO framework. No amount of refinement to the heuristic vector itself will yield meaningful gains beyond ~35.82. Instead, the path forward lies unequivocally in **shifting from heuristic content to heuristic orchestration**. Future breakthroughs must emerge from colony-level adaptive mechanisms that leverage collective intelligence without compromising signal clarity at the item level.

Promising directions now crystallize into three actionable strategies: (a) **Norm-switching ant colonies**, where subsets of ants use different norms (e.g., exploratory ants use L∞ for feasibility, exploitative ants use L1 for density), coordinated via pheromone thresholds or construction phase; (b) **Confidence-modulated pheromone integration**, where the influence of pheromone trails is dynamically adjusted based on the discriminative score gap—high confidence (large gap) → strong heuristic dominance, low confidence (small gap) → greater reliance on pheromone history; and (c) **Lightweight interaction proxies embedded directly in the solution construction policy**, such as temporary exclusion of high-conflict items or local re-ranking based on partial assignments, rather than attempting to precompute complex relationships in a static vector.

In summary, the research has matured into a clear and empirically validated paradigm: maintain a minimal, pure library of efficiency norms; select one decisively per instance using the proven discriminative score; apply moderate, variance-scaled power transformation; and delegate all adaptivity to the *colony’s dynamic decision-making process*. The next leap will not come from making the heuristic smarter, but from making the colony wiser about how—and when—to use it.
