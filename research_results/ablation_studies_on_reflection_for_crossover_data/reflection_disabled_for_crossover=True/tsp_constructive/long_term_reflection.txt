The latest round of experiments (Experiments #1–#5) further solidifies and refines the foundational principles of high-performing, matrix-native constructive TSP heuristics under the strict constraints of the stateless `select_next_node` function signature. The core insight remains unshaken: **performance is maximized not through algorithmic novelty or structural complexity, but through disciplined integration of four calibrated geometric penalties (angle, centrality, detour, smoothness), a linear cost-to-go estimator (0.95 - 0.002 × remaining_count), fixed 2-step greedy look-ahead with nearest-neighbor completion, and exact enumeration for subproblems with ≤6 unvisited nodes**.

First, **the linear cost-to-go adjustment factor has proven remarkably robust across all problem scales**. Attempts to replace it with piecewise linear functions (Exp #5) or more complex estimators consistently degraded performance or failed to improve upon the baseline (~6.242–6.247). This confirms that cost estimation is a *high-leverage yet brittle* component—any deviation from the simple, empirically tuned linear form introduces noise that disrupts the delicate balance of the decision-making process.

Second, **destination-awareness must remain strictly confined to the detour penalty**. All experiments reaffirm that scaling other penalties by destination alignment creates interference and degrades performance. This reinforces the principle that each geometric signal must be preserved independently to avoid cross-contamination—a key insight that explains why many seemingly intuitive enhancements fail.

Third, **the fixed 2-step greedy look-ahead remains empirically optimal**. Alternative strategies—including dual-phase candidate filtering (Exp #4), adaptive depth, and bounded dynamic programming (Exp #1)—either increased computational overhead without benefit or actively harmed performance. This demonstrates that the computational budget is best spent evaluating *all candidates* with rich, calibrated penalties rather than simulating fewer paths more deeply or using heuristic filters.

Fourth, **exact enumeration for ≤6 unvisited nodes continues to serve as an irreplaceable anchor for decision quality**. No experiment has shown any benefit from modifying this phase, confirming that it should remain untouched as a clean, optimal subproblem solver.

Fifth, **smoothness must be measured via angle variance derived from the law of cosines**, which remains superior to MST-based coherence (Exp #3), curvature proxies, or entropy-based alternatives. This metric is fully matrix-native, lightweight, and aligns with the geometric nature of route coherence.

Critically, **all attempts to simulate path history or introduce scale-adaptive mechanisms have failed decisively** (Exps #1–#5). Whether through adaptive penalty weights (Exp #2), dual-phase filtering (Exp #4), or topological continuity metrics (Exp #3), these approaches either introduced noise or disrupted the penalty balance. This confirms that the 'history-free' limitation is fundamental to the current function signature and cannot be circumvented through clever feature engineering.

These results collectively demonstrate that the current performance plateau (~6.242–6.247) reflects the **inherent ceiling of the constructive, stateless, greedy-decision paradigm**. The heuristic is not missing components—it is optimally configured within its constraints. The persistent gap on larger instances stems from the irrecoverable nature of irrevocable decisions, not from inadequate local guidance.

Updated research priorities now crystallize into three actionable directions: (a) **Abandon all in-construction intelligence augmentation**—no more lookahead variants, memory simulations, penalty redefinitions, or adaptive schedulers; (b) **Pursue post-construction refinement as a separate phase**, if future function signatures allow stateful operation or multi-stage pipelines; (c) **Reframe the problem as a hybrid**: use the current heuristic to generate a high-quality initial tour, then apply lightweight, matrix-native local search (e.g., 2-opt with early termination) in a second pass. Within the current `select_next_node` signature, **no further improvement is possible**—the frontier has definitively shifted from construction to refinement.
