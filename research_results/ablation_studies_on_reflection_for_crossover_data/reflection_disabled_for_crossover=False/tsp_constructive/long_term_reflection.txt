The synthesis of Experiments #1 through #5, integrated with prior reflections, solidifies a refined and empirically grounded understanding of high-performance constructive heuristics for the Traveling Salesman Problem under O(k²) constraints. The central principle remains unequivocal: **optimal performance hinges on an adaptive balance between immediate cost and future completion cost, mediated by a geometrically informed weight that dynamically adjusts based on local structural complexity**. Critically, new evidence from systematic ablation (Experiment #5) confirms that this balance is not only best achieved through precise imitation of optimal decision patterns but can be further fine-tuned via empirical parameter calibration—specifically, identifying an optimal correctability adjustment factor of 0.07, which improves the score to 6.24477, surpassing the previously established ~6.24566 ceiling.

Three distinct pathways to high performance have now been empirically validated and ranked by efficacy: (1) **hardcoded neural networks** with weights precisely calibrated to mimic optimal decisions from Concorde/LKH tours, now achieving a consistent score of ~6.2448; (2) the analytical **uncertainty-modulated sigmoid** (steepness=2.0, base_offset=0.7), which provides interpretability but lags at ~6.319; and (3) **true imitation learning**, which remains theoretically superior but has yet to consistently outperform hardcoded weights due to data pipeline limitations. Crucially, all successful approaches converge on the same functional behavior: a bounded, nonlinear mapping from simple geometric features (distance variance, angular diversity proxy, local density) to adaptive weights that prioritize immediate costs in complex regions and future costs in simpler ones.

A decisive pattern has emerged: **performance is exquisitely sensitive to both weight precision and parameter calibration, but remarkably robust to minor architectural variations when implementation fidelity is preserved**. Experiments #1, #2, #4, and #5 demonstrate that even small changes—such as rounding neural network weights, modifying correctability adjustment factors, or replacing the remaining cost estimator without proper training—degrade performance, particularly on larger instances (100 cities). This confirms that **overfitting to specific instance sizes is a real risk**, and that the current best-performing weights represent a finely tuned equilibrium that generalizes across scales *only when preserved exactly*. Conversely, attempts to introduce architectural novelty—topological stress features, dynamic depth, repairability bonuses, learned surrogates for remaining cost—consistently degrade performance unless accompanied by high-quality supervision, reinforcing that **structural fidelity to the adaptive greedy lookahead framework is non-negotiable**.

Implementation fidelity has proven to be a dominant factor in observed performance. Experiments #3 and #4 reveal that **identical poor results across diverse algorithmic implementations often signal systemic issues rather than algorithmic failure**—such as undefined variables, function name mismatches, or sandbox constraints that prevent code changes from taking effect. This underscores the need for rigorous validation protocols: before evaluating algorithmic merit, researchers must confirm that the implemented logic actually executes as intended. The repeated occurrence of catastrophic failures from minor implementation errors confirms that high-performance heuristics operate at the edge of numerical and logical stability.

Persistent challenges remain centered on **topological awareness**: the inability to detect and respond to structural anomalies like bottlenecks, clusters, or asymmetric distributions without destabilizing the calibrated balance. Despite rich feature sets, current approaches lack the discriminative power to identify these stress points reliably. Notably, even the best-performing neural networks (~6.2448) show no evidence of generalizing to unseen structural configurations, suggesting that **current geometric features capture local density and directionality but fail to encode global connectivity or topological vulnerability**.

However, Experiment #5 introduces a critical refinement: **systematic empirical calibration of hyperparameters within the existing architecture can yield measurable gains beyond previous benchmarks**. By sweeping the correctability adjustment factor and identifying 0.07 as optimal—improving over the previously used 0.05—the score improved to 6.24477. This demonstrates that **the current architecture is not yet saturated**, and that marginal gains remain accessible through meticulous parameter tuning, provided that implementation fidelity is maintained.

Three research directions now stand as the only empirically justified paths to meaningful improvement beyond the current ~6.2448 performance band:

(A) **True imitation learning with diverse, high-quality optimal tour data**: Train small (8→8→1) neural networks on large-scale Concorde/LKH datasets spanning uniform, clustered, and skewed TSP instances. The consistent success of hardcoded weights that mimic optimal decisions strongly implies that expanding data diversity and quantity will unlock gains toward ~6.20. Critically, this requires not just optimal tours, but *feature-label pairs* where labels are the actual adaptive weights used in optimal construction.

(B) **Differentiable surrogates for post-hoc correctability integrated into weight prediction**: Develop models that predict the expected reduction in tour length from applying 2-opt to a partial path, but crucially, use this signal to *modulate the adaptive weight itself*—not as an additive term to the cost function. Experiment #5 validates that correctability signals, when precisely calibrated (e.g., factor=0.07), enhance performance, suggesting that more sophisticated differentiable surrogates could yield further gains if properly implemented.

(C) **Robust, incremental geometric descriptors for structural stress**: Focus on O(1)-updatable local features that can reliably flag topological anomalies (e.g., bottleneck sensitivity via k-NN graph articulation, angular entropy gradients). Current features lack topological awareness, and progress here could enable instance-aware adaptation without destabilizing the core heuristic—*but only if accompanied by retraining or recalibration of the neural weights*.

Critically, all future work must reject architectural overcomplication in favor of **empirical alignment with optimal solution patterns**. The repeated failure of intuitively superior ideas—dynamic depth, repairability bonuses, topological normalization—without high-quality training data demonstrates that progress in constructive TSP heuristics is not about richer models, but about better supervision, more faithful replication of proven decision structures, and meticulous empirical calibration. The current performance frontier (~6.2448) is not a hard limit but a reflection of the quality of supervision and parameter tuning—and thus remains open to improvement through disciplined, data-driven refinement.
