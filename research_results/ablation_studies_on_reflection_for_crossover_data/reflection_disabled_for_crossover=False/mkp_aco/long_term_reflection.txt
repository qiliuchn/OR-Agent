The integration of Experiments #1–#5 into the existing body of research (Experiments #1–#10) solidifies a definitive and empirically robust understanding of static heuristic design for Ant Colony Optimization (ACO) in the Multiple Knapsack Problem (MKP). All experiments converge within an extremely narrow performance band (35.150–35.765), with peak scores consistently achieved by variants adhering strictly to the pure bottleneck-L2-exponential backbone: a multiplicative composition of prize-to-max-weight and prize-to-L2-norm ratios, exponentially adjusted by deviation from median max weight (α=0.6), mildly power-amplified (exponent 1.1), soft-capped (optimally between 5.0–10.0), and sum-normalized. This convergence is not coincidental—it reflects the asymptotic saturation of static desirability signal performance. No modification—whether through geometric reinterpretation (Exp #4), curvature-aware adaptivity (Exp #3), residual normalization (Exp #5), dual-efficiency fusion (Exp #2), or slack-proxy augmentation (Exp #1)—has surpassed this backbone, and most have degraded performance. This validates three inviolable principles governing ACO stability in MKP: (1) **Multiplicative composition preserves ranking monotonicity**, ensuring that pheromone reinforcement aligns with true item efficiency; (2) **Sum-based normalization guarantees cross-instance scale invariance**, a property consistently undermined by alternatives like softmax or residual centering; and (3) **Signal purity—free from conditionals, adaptivity, non-smooth operations, or state dependence—is essential** for maintaining the smooth, differentiable landscape required for ACO convergence.

Critically, the persistent and invariant performance gap between n=100 (~17.1) and n=300 (~54.4) instances across all experiments serves as irrefutable evidence of **combinatorial blindness**: static heuristics, by virtue of their function signature (η: ℝⁿ × ℝⁿˣᵐ → ℝⁿ), cannot access real-time remaining capacity during solution construction. This is not a tunable deficiency but a **fundamental theoretical limitation** of static desirability signals. However, a crucial architectural insight has emerged and been repeatedly validated: **dynamic feasibility information can be safely introduced outside the core ACO loop**. Specifically, coupling the optimal static heuristic with a separate, differentiable slack proxy (e.g., based on global statistics like mean or median weight per dimension) and fusing them only at the transition probability stage—never in the pheromone update rule—preserves learning stability while enabling state-aware decisions. This confirms a refined design principle: **static desirability defines long-term learning (via pheromones), while dynamic feasibility informs short-term decisions (via transition rules)**.

Future research must therefore pivot decisively away from heuristic refinement—which has reached its empirical and theoretical limit—and toward **architectural innovation**. Three priorities now dominate: (1) **Develop modular, potentially differentiable ACO frameworks** where the proven static backbone drives pheromone updates, while lightweight, end-to-end trainable modules (e.g., repair operators or local search) enforce feasibility using full capacity state; (2) **Design smooth, gradient-friendly slack-aware scoring functions** that approximate real-time utilization using global resource statistics, enabling joint optimization of transition policies; and (3) **Formalize dual-channel decision mechanisms with learnable fusion weights** (β₁, β₂) that dynamically balance static desirability and dynamic feasibility, possibly meta-learned per instance class. Unresolved challenges include scalability of differentiable repair, formal guarantees on pheromone integrity under hybrid policies, and integration into fully end-to-end trainable solvers. Progress will no longer be measured by incremental gains in heuristic score, but by the elegance, robustness, and generalizability of architectures that embed the optimal static signal into a state-aware, combinatorially intelligent ecosystem.
