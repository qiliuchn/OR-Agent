The cumulative evidence from twenty experiments—including breakthrough results from Experiments #12, #17, and #19–#20, now reinforced by consistent findings across Experiments #1–#10—solidifies a refined paradigm for heuristic design in Ant Colony Optimization (ACO) applied to the Multiple Knapsack Problem (MKP). The central principle remains unshaken: **prize divided by max-weight per item is the optimal base heuristic**, consistently outperforming alternatives such as L2 norms, average weights, constraint-tightness-weighted sums, or multi-signal adaptive combinations. This robustness stems from its ability to directly penalize items that risk violating any single capacity constraint—a critical bottleneck-aware signal in multidimensional packing.

Crucially, recent experiments confirm that **complexity without structural alignment degrades performance**. Attempts to incorporate resource scarcity (#1, #2, #9), dynamic dimension weighting (#2, #8, #10), or bottleneck-specific competition modeling (#4, #5, #7) either underperformed or matched simpler baselines only after reverting to core components. Notably, every successful variant ultimately converged on three elements: (1) prize/max_weight as the base desirability, (2) cosine similarity over full weight vectors to measure competition, and (3) exponential diversity discounts applied selectively to high-value items (e.g., prize > median), with fixed parameters (threshold=0.1, exponent=1.4, bottleneck coefficient=−0.5). This triad forms a *minimal effective architecture*—any deviation risks fragility, implementation error, or performance loss.

A key new insight emerging across experiments is that **global geometric similarity (via cosine) implicitly encodes feasible co-selection better than explicit bottleneck overlap or conflict graphs**. Cosine similarity captures angular relationships in weight space, which correlate with whether two items can coexist without jointly saturating multiple constraints—a pattern that min-overlap (#6), L0-sparsity (#9), or utilization-thresholded dimensions (#5, #7) fail to model holistically. This explains why even well-intentioned bottleneck-aware mechanisms underperform: they fragment the multidimensional interaction into local views, losing the global feasibility geometry.

Furthermore, the persistent **n=100 vs. n=300 performance trade-off** is not an artifact to be engineered away but a fundamental characteristic of static heuristics in ACO. Hybrid switching (#6), adaptive thresholds (#10), or instance-size-specific penalties consistently failed to resolve it, reinforcing that static heuristics lack the contextual awareness needed for scale-invariant optimization. Instead, the highest scores (#17’s 35.488, #6’s 35.480) came from *multi-scenario simulation ensembles*—non-deterministic greedy packings under varied orders and weightings—that implicitly sample combinatorial contention patterns across scales without explicit size detection.

Recent experiments (#6, #7) have pushed the performance ceiling to **35.48–35.49**, confirming that **parameter tuning within the minimal effective architecture can yield marginal but meaningful gains**. Specifically, slightly stronger penalties (competition exponent = -1.6, bottleneck = -0.7) improved large-instance performance without catastrophic small-instance degradation, suggesting the original parent parameters were conservative. However, attempts to replace exponential discounting with learned models (#6, #10) or multiplicative interactions (#2, #4) either failed or required reverting to exponential forms, underscoring the **numerical stability and interpretability of exponential penalties** as a critical success factor.

Three enduring challenges remain: (a) the **performance ceiling near 35.49**, suggesting diminishing returns from static proxy refinement; (b) **extreme sensitivity to implementation details and stochasticity**, where minor changes (e.g., seed fixes, normalization epsilon) cause measurable drops; and (c) the **architectural blockage against true dynamic adaptation**, as ACO evaluates heuristics only once at initialization, preventing integration of residual capacity during construction.

These insights crystallize a strategic pivot: the era of static heuristic engineering is maturing, and future breakthroughs require **algorithmic co-design**. Three updated research directions now take priority: **(1) Framework-aware dynamic heuristics**—modify ACO to pass knapsack residual capacities to the heuristic function during ant construction, enabling real-time slack-aware desirability updates; **(2) Scenario-enhanced static proxies**—refine multi-scenario simulation with principled scenario selection (e.g., k-adaptive, dimension-decoupled packings) and investigate why simple averaging across scenarios outperforms learned fusion; and **(3) Interaction-focused penalty design**—explore joint formulations where competition similarity and bottleneck consumption interact multiplicatively, potentially using lightweight learned weights rather than fixed exponents, while preserving numerical stability. The path forward lies not in more complex static formulas, but in harmonizing heuristic intelligence with the algorithm’s temporal structure.
