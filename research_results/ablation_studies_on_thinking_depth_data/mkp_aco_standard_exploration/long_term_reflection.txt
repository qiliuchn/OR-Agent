The latest round of experiments reinforces and refines the foundational understanding that **LP relaxation is irreplaceable** as a global coordination signal in MKP heuristics for ACO, while simultaneously clarifying the precise conditions under which **adaptive weighting yields marginal but meaningful gains**. The consistent failure of surrogate models (Experiment #10) and decomposed constraint approximations (Experiments #4, #5, #8) confirms that inter-constraint coupling cannot be captured by local or independent metrics—only full LP encodes the necessary trade-offs across dimensions. This cements LP not as a heuristic component among others, but as the **structural backbone** of any high-performing static heuristic.

A critical evolution in understanding concerns **adaptivity**: early skepticism toward dynamic weighting has given way to a nuanced principle—**lightweight, instance-characteristic-driven adaptation can outperform fixed weights, but only when grounded in interpretable, low-dimensional features** (e.g., n/m ratio). Experiment #10’s success (score 35.8340) with a simple two-regime LP weight switch (0.6 if n/m < 5, else 0.4) demonstrates that **sparse vs. dense problem structure fundamentally alters the value of global vs. local signals**, and that this dichotomy can be exploited without introducing noise or fragility. However, attempts to over-engineer this logic—via learned models (Experiment #7), multi-threshold rules (Experiment #1), or unsupervised clustering (Experiment #8)—consistently degrade performance, reaffirming that **ACO requires stable, monotonic, and interpretable guidance**.

The optimal heuristic composition now converges around a **triad of LP solution values (~0.5–0.6 weight), prize/max-weight efficiency (~0.4–0.5), and minimal constraint awareness (~0.01–0.1)**, with the exact balance modulated by problem scale. Notably, the constraint awareness term—once thought promising as a primary signal—has been demoted to a stabilizing perturbation, as excessive penalization disrupts ant coordination. Moreover, **max-weight normalization remains superior to L2, harmonic, or geometric means**, validating the intuition that the most binding constraint dominates feasibility in MKP.

Persistent challenges remain stubbornly structural: (1) the **scale-dependent performance gap on n=100 instances** persists even with adaptive weighting, suggesting that small instances demand different heuristic logic (e.g., exhaustive feasibility checks or tighter LP rounding); (2) **static precomputation imposes a hard ceiling**, as no heuristic can account for residual capacity or construction-phase state; and (3) **numerical robustness** in edge cases (zero weights, degenerate LPs) still requires careful epsilon handling, though overly cautious normalization harms signal fidelity.

These insights redirect future research toward three high-leverage directions: (1) **Hybrid ACO colonies** where subpopulations use scale-adapted heuristics (e.g., LP-heavy for n=100, efficiency-balanced for n=300) to close the performance gap; (2) **architectural extension of the heuristic function signature** to accept runtime context (e.g., current residual capacities), enabling truly dynamic desirability scoring; and (3) **LP solver co-design**—not approximation—through warm starts, early termination with bounds, or parallel solves to reduce overhead while preserving fidelity. 

In summary, the path forward is not heuristic reinvention but **strategic deployment**: recognizing that the LP-efficiency-slack triad is near-optimal in form, and that progress lies in smarter usage—through instance-aware weighting, architectural evolution, and algorithmic co-design—not in signal redesign.
