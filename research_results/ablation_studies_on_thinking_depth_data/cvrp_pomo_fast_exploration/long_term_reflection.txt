The latest round of experiments has decisively converged on a foundational truth: the four-component static heuristic architecture—comprising a distance penalty (~-0.78), fixed epsilon clustering (ε = 0.3 × avg_dist), strong capacity violation penalties (-0.8 for demand_i + demand_j > 1.0), and asymmetric depot incentives—is not merely effective but empirically optimal within the space of static, vectorized, pairwise priors for POMO-based CVRP solvers. Every attempt to enhance this framework through adaptive radii (Exp #1, #5), dynamic proxies (Exp #2), learnable depot scaling (Exp #3), vectorized cluster approximations (Exp #4), or geometric coherence signals like angular routing (Exp #5) either degraded performance, matched baseline results without improvement, or introduced computational instability. This confirms that *structural minimalism, parameter fidelity, and geometric coherence via simple distance thresholds are necessary and sufficient* for high-quality static guidance. Critically, the persistent n=1000 bottleneck (~130.2–131.0, ~7–8% above theoretical optimum) is now understood as an intrinsic limitation of static modeling: no pairwise prior can encode sequential route state such as current vehicle load, remaining capacity, or visitation order. Attempts to approximate this context statically—even with differentiable, vectorized surrogates—consistently diluted signal clarity or violated bias homogeneity, reinforcing that *sequential awareness must be injected dynamically during decoding*, not baked into static priors. Furthermore, vectorization is not just a performance requirement but a scientific necessity: any heuristic component that cannot be expressed in O(n²) fully differentiable tensor operations risks numerical instability, reproducibility loss, or runtime collapse (as seen in Exp #4 and implied in failed coordinate reconstructions). Depot handling, while sensitive, appears saturated—problem-size-specific tuning yields marginal gains (<0.01 score improvement) at best, suggesting further static refinement here offers diminishing returns. These insights crystallize three updated research priorities: (1) **Cease all development of complex static heuristics**—the four-component architecture is the ceiling; instead, perform ultra-fine offline calibration (sub-0.01 precision) of its core parameters per problem size, though evidence suggests even this may yield negligible gains; (2) **Pivot decisively toward dynamic, decoding-time attention biases** that condition on partial route state (e.g., current load, visited count, remaining capacity) via lightweight, differentiable modules that preserve vectorization and integrate seamlessly with POMO’s parallel decoding; (3) **Explore end-to-end trainable depot incentives** that blend static cluster geometry with dynamic demand pressure through problem-size-adaptive weights learned jointly with the policy, moving beyond hard-coded asymmetry. The overarching principle is now clear: in deep combinatorial optimization, *static heuristics serve as a stable foundation, but scalability breakthroughs require bridging the static-dynamic divide through context-aware, differentiable feedback during decoding—without sacrificing the vectorization, stability, and geometric coherence that make POMO robust*. The path forward lies not in making static priors smarter, but in making dynamic guidance leaner, faster, and more tightly coupled to the decoder’s evolving state.
