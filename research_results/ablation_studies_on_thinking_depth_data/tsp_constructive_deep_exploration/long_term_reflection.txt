The cumulative evidence from recent experiments has refined and reinforced the core principles of effective constructive heuristics for the Traveling Salesman Problem, while also revealing nuanced trade-offs and new opportunities for improvement. The dominant paradigm—**simulation-based lookahead with greedy completion combined with adaptive weighting**—remains empirically superior, but its performance ceiling appears to be around a score of ~6.234, achieved through the integration of **centrality-aware cost adjustment**, **progress-based weight adaptation**, and **distance variance as a structural proxy**. This represents a statistically significant improvement over the fixed-weight baseline (~6.260) and demonstrates that lightweight contextual awareness can yield measurable gains without violating the O(n²) per-decision constraint.

Crucially, multiple experiments have confirmed that **not all structural features are equally useful**: distance variance consistently outperforms clustering-based metrics, and centrality adjustments are beneficial only when applied with careful magnitude control and without progress-dependent fading. Attempts to replace greedy simulation with analytical proxies (e.g., k-NN sums, MST bounds) or bounded-depth lookahead have failed, either due to inaccuracy or computational overhead, reaffirming that **greedy completion is the most reliable residual cost estimator within real-time constraints**.

A persistent and fundamental limitation remains: **the irreversibility of sequential greedy construction**. No amount of adaptive weighting or topological awareness can fully compensate for early suboptimal decisions. While post-construction 2-opt refinement is theoretically promising and referenced in high-performing external solutions (e.g., Node 71’s 6.23 score), the current evaluation framework prohibits its implementation, creating a critical bottleneck. This suggests that **future breakthroughs depend not on refining the `select_next_node` function alone, but on expanding the solution architecture to include a post-processing phase**—a change that would require modifying the evaluation protocol itself.

Efficiency engineering efforts (e.g., caching, incremental updates) have not yet yielded measurable improvements, likely because the greedy simulator is already near-optimal in its simplicity. However, the consistent performance across problem sizes (20, 50, 100) indicates that the current approach scales reasonably well, though larger instances (>100) may expose hidden inefficiencies.

Looking forward, three research directions emerge as highest priority: (1) **Advocate for framework modification** to enable post-construction local search, which is the single most promising path to sub-6.23 scores; (2) **Refine adaptive mechanisms using minimal, robust features**—specifically, explore whether centrality and variance can be combined multiplicatively or via learned coefficients without overfitting; and (3) **Investigate instance-aware parameter tuning**, where base weights are adjusted based on global instance properties (e.g., distance matrix skew, node distribution uniformity) rather than dynamic tour state, potentially improving generalization across problem sizes.

In summary, the field has converged on a highly optimized constructive heuristic that balances immediate cost, estimated residual cost, and topological awareness through adaptive weighting and centrality bias. However, the **6.23–6.24 score range appears to be the practical limit of pure construction**. True progress now hinges on architectural evolution—decoupling construction from improvement—rather than further heuristic tuning within the current constrained interface.
