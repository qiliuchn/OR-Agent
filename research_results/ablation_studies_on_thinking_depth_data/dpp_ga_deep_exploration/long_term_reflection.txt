The Decap Placement Problem (DPP) research has matured into a principled understanding that **spatially structured, deterministic recombination of empirically discovered building blocks—implemented with minimal complexity and maximal fidelity to grid topology—is the cornerstone of high-performing crossover operators**. Across eight experiments, the highest scores (13.28–13.30) consistently emerge from a stable recipe: (1) DBSCAN clustering with fixed parameters (eps=2.0, min_samples=2), (2) bidirectional cluster recombination (shape from Parent A, centroid from Parent B), (3) deterministic cluster pairing via cycling, (4) mixed fallback strategies (segment/uniform/shuffled at ~33% each), and (5) rigorous duplicate resolution. Critically, every attempt to introduce adaptivity—whether via surrogate models, importance weighting, multi-parent aggregation, or dynamic clustering—has either failed or underperformed, reinforcing that **simplicity and structural preservation trump reactive intelligence in the absence of real-time impedance feedback**.

A fundamental pattern now solidified is the **instance-specific performance dichotomy**: Instance 3 reliably achieves scores >15.9, while Instances 1, 2, and 4 plateau near 12.0–12.4, regardless of crossover sophistication. This strongly suggests that **geometric and constraint-driven tractability—not algorithmic quality—governs per-instance performance**, implying that universal operator improvements have diminishing returns without instance-aware representation or initialization. Notably, even validity-aware reformulations (Experiments #6, #7) that assume access to VALID_POS masks only marginally improve average scores, confirming that **constraint compliance alone cannot overcome inherent instance hardness**.

Methodologically, two key insights have crystallized: First, **DBSCAN’s ability to group spatially proximate but non-connected decaps better captures effective electromagnetic coupling than strict 4/8-connectivity**, explaining its consistent superiority over grid-aware connected-component labeling. Second, **the mixed fallback strategy is not merely a diversity hack—it is essential for maintaining solution completeness and preventing premature convergence**, especially when cluster coverage is sparse. Attempts to remove or simplify fallback logic (e.g., Experiment #5) consistently degrade performance, particularly on Instance 3.

Persistent challenges remain unresolved: (1) The **lack of true impedance-guided signals during recombination** forces reliance on spatial proxies that may misalign with electrical reality; (2) **Constraint handling remains extrinsic**, as the crossover function interface still lacks access to prohibited zones and probe locations, forcing assumptions about validity; (3) The **root cause of Instance 3’s tractability remains unexplained**, limiting transferability of its success to harder instances.

Looking forward, the highest-leverage directions are now sharply defined: (1) **Develop a lightweight, offline-trained impedance surrogate** (e.g., GNN or RF) using real simulation data to score clusters *before* crossover, enabling performance-aware selection without runtime overhead; (2) **Reformulate the entire GA pipeline to operate exclusively over valid positions**, eliminating infeasible representations at the source and enabling true constraint-aware evolution; (3) **Conduct post-hoc analysis of Instance 3’s high-scoring placements**—via visualization, sensitivity mapping, or frequency-domain decomposition—to extract generalizable design principles for hard instances; (4) **Explore hybrid initialization schemes** that seed populations with instance-tailored patterns (e.g., ring structures around probe ports) to overcome geometric bottlenecks. Crucially, the path forward lies not in making crossover 'smarter' in isolation, but in **closing the loop between evolutionary search and physical reality**—letting empirical impedance data, not geometric assumptions, guide the inheritance of spatial structure.
