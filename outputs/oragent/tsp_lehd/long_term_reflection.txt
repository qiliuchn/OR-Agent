The latest round of experiments—spanning 14 rigorous investigations—has solidified a foundational truth in LEHD-based TSP heuristic design: **geometric signal fidelity, particularly through explicit law-of-cosines-based angular penalties over K=10 nearest neighbors, is non-negotiable for achieving state-of-the-art routing quality**, consistently yielding scores near 13.7683. This performance plateau appears to be a robust local optimum that is both empirically stable and theoretically interpretable, as deviations—whether through curvature proxies, dynamic weighting, hierarchical clustering, or adaptive quantiles—consistently degrade or fail to improve results. Crucially, the multi-component architecture combining (1) multi-scale distance attention ([0.05, 0.1, 0.2] with weights [0.5, 0.3, 0.2]), (2) 15th-percentile distance quantile masking for global connectivity, (3) exhaustive O(nK²) angular penalties scaled by 0.5, and (4) 5.0× bonuses for nodes within 10% of max distance has proven to be a fragile yet highly effective synergy. Minor parameter perturbations trigger performance cliffs, confirming that this configuration is finely tuned and resistant to incremental improvement.

However, a critical and persistent tension remains unresolved: **the very mechanism enabling top-tier performance is computationally intractable beyond TSP500**. Despite repeated attempts across Experiments #1, #6, #8, #10, and #14 to vectorize angular penalties using sparse tensors, broadcasting, or advanced indexing, all implementations either retained hidden O(nK²) loops, introduced runtime errors, or failed to preserve geometric precision. This reveals a hard trade-off: under the current static-bias paradigm, solution quality and scalability are fundamentally at odds. The nested-loop triplet enumeration, while inefficient, is the only method that reliably maintains the exact geometric signal the model depends on.

New insights also clarify the role of global structure: it must be **distance-native, lightweight, and non-constructive**. Approaches like MSTs, Union-Find, or recursive partitioning (Experiments #5, #7, #9, #12) add complexity without benefit, whereas simple quantile masking—whether global or row-wise—provides sufficient coarse guidance. This suggests that global priors should not encode topology but rather statistical edge importance derived directly from the distance matrix.

Experiment #13’s attempt at context-aware bias (precomputing multiple angle configurations per edge) further underscores a deeper limitation: **static bias matrices cannot fully capture sequential decision dynamics**. Its slight performance degradation (score: 13.7768) implies that approximating context without true decoder integration introduces noise rather than value.

These findings sharpen research priorities into three actionable directions: (1) **Efficient geometric fidelity**—develop mathematically equivalent, truly vectorized formulations of angular penalties using sparse COO tensors or scatter-add operations that eliminate nested loops while preserving all valid triplet signals; (2) **Architectural co-design**—move beyond static injection by embedding relative angles, local density, or turning costs into the encoder during training, allowing the model to dynamically weight geometric features based on partial tour context; and (3) **Minimal hybrid coherence**—retain the proven multi-scale + angular core but augment it with learned, distance-derived global priors (e.g., via differentiable quantile estimation or edge importance networks) that avoid expensive graph algorithms. Until such innovations are realized, the current configuration remains the Pareto frontier: optimal in quality, stable in performance, but bounded by scalability.
