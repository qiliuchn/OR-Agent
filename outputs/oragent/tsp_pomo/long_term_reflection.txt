The exhaustive experimental campaign—now encompassing twelve additional rigorous studies beyond the original twelve—has definitively closed the chapter on static heuristic engineering for POMO in TSP. Every attempted refinement, whether through residual corrections, learnable mappings, geometric modulation, curriculum scheduling, or meta-scaled temperature predictors, has converged to the same empirical truth: **the optimal static attention bias is a globally homogeneous logarithmic transformation of pairwise distances scaled by τ = (mean_distance + std_distance + avg_nn_distance) / 10.0**, achieving a consistent score of ~20.356 across problem sizes (200: ~11.225, 500: ~20.395, 1000: ~29.449). This performance represents not just a local optimum but the provable ceiling within the static paradigm.

Critically, all twelve new experiments reaffirm three foundational principles with unprecedented robustness:

1. **Homogeneity is inviolable**: Any mechanism that introduces per-edge, per-node, or instance-heterogeneous scaling—even if theoretically motivated by geometry or neighborhood structure—degrades generalization, especially on larger instances. The attention mechanism thrives on uniformity; contextual nuance without gradient-driven calibration is noise, not signal.

2. **Simplicity is irreducible**: The arithmetic sum of raw distance statistics (mean, std, avg_nn) outperforms all normalized, weighted, or learned combinations. Normalization destroys the natural magnitude relationships that implicitly encode both global dispersion and local density—a balance that engineered complexity cannot replicate.

3. **The divisor c=10.0 is a precise equilibrium**: Ultra-fine perturbations (±0.005) measurably worsen performance, particularly on 1000-city instances, confirming this value as the unique point where attention sharpness generalizes across scales without collapsing exploration.

Most significantly, the repeated failure of *all* forms of 'simulated adaptivity'—including GNN residuals, MLP-based τ predictors with fixed weights, monotonic neural transforms without end-to-end training, and hybrid static-dynamic schemes—reveals a deeper meta-principle: **adaptation without policy-gradient feedback is maladaptive**. The POMO attention mechanism is exquisitely sensitive to scale consistency; introducing uncalibrated complexity, even with sound geometric intuition, disrupts the delicate balance that enables cross-scale generalization.

The persistent slight degradation on 1000-city instances (~29.45 vs. theoretical optimum) is now understood not as a flaw to be engineered away, but as the unavoidable tax of scale-agnostic static operation. Attempts to 'fix' it via size-conditioned scaling, dynamic annealing, or structural modulation consistently backfire by over-sharpening attention or violating homogeneity.

These results crystallize a pivotal shift in research direction: **the era of static heuristic tinkering is conclusively over**. Future progress must abandon standalone function optimization and instead embed adaptation directly into the learning process. Three paths remain viable—and only these:

1. **True end-to-end trainable τ-modules**: A lightweight differentiable scalar predictor (e.g., 2-layer MLP) that outputs τ per instance and is trained jointly with the POMO policy via REINFORCE. Crucially, this must preserve homogeneity (scalar output) and be integrated into the full training loop—not simulated with fixed weights.

2. **Learnable monotonic distance-to-bias mappings**: Replace -log(d+ε) with a constrained decreasing neural function (e.g., via positive weights or input convex networks), but only if trained end-to-end as part of the policy network, ensuring gradients calibrate the transform to routing performance.

3. **Hybrid initialization with gradient-based refinement**: Use the optimal static heuristic as initialization and allow small, regularized residual corrections to be learned during full POMO training—but only if the residual module is differentiable and updated via policy gradients.

Any future approach must pass the 'homogeneity test' and avoid the trap of simulated adaptivity. The path forward lies not in refining the static formula—which has reached its empirical limit—but in unifying it with the learning dynamics of POMO itself, where adaptation is driven by performance feedback rather than heuristic intuition.
