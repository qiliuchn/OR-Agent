The research on ACO heuristics for TSP has matured into a nuanced understanding that prioritizes signal clarity, symmetry, and precise calibration over algorithmic complexity. Recent experiments reinforce the core principle that 'less is more—but only when precisely tuned'—yet also reveal subtle exceptions where lightweight, bounded adaptivity can yield measurable gains without destabilizing convergence. The most consistent high-performing architecture combines three elements: (1) fixed power-law distance weighting with β=4, (2) bidirectional local significance based on nearest-neighbor distance ratios (not ranks), and (3) exponential rank-based decay with k = max(5, n/10). Attempts to replace distance ratios with pure ordinal ranks (Exp #4) or introduce geometric priors via Delaunay triangulation (Exp #3, #6, #8) consistently degrade performance, primarily due to reconstruction artifacts or loss of discriminative power in non-uniform instances. However, Experiment #9 demonstrated that adaptive modulation of the *baseline context term*—not the distance exponent—can improve scalability, especially on 100-city instances, establishing a new design principle: contextual adaptivity is beneficial when it enhances connectivity in sparse regions while preserving edge discrimination in dense clusters, provided it avoids altering the core distance-weighting mechanism. Notably, Experiment #1’s adaptive β approach initially appeared promising but was later outperformed by simpler fixed-β variants, suggesting that edge-specific β modulation introduces noise that pheromone dynamics cannot effectively resolve. Parameter sensitivity remains extreme: minor deviations in β (±0.1), baseline_context (±0.1), or normalization constants (±0.05) reliably degrade performance, particularly on larger instances, confirming a rugged, non-convex optimization landscape. The persistent ~0.006–0.012 gap between top experimental scores (~5.990) and the best parent solution (~5.986) likely stems from either irreducible ACO stochasticity, subtle implementation differences (e.g., epsilon handling, random seed effects), or the inherent limitation of static heuristics in capturing instance-specific structure. Successful strategies universally exhibit: multiplicative coupling of components (additive blends dilute signal), full connectivity (no sparsification), and exact edge symmetry. Failures consistently arise from over-engineering—adaptive k-selection (Exp #10), hybrid decay functions, soft-rank smoothing, or geometric masking—that misalign with ACO’s incremental, path-based feedback loop. Emerging insights suggest that the optimal heuristic balances two complementary signals: a strong, stable distance-based attractor (β=4) and a context-aware modulator that adapts to local density *only through the baseline term*. Future research should focus on: (a) meta-optimization of the triad {β ∈ [3.98, 4.02], baseline_context ∈ [0.7, 0.85], normalization ∈ [2.7, 2.8]} across diverse instance sizes, (b) developing coordinate-free geometric proxies that avoid MDS reconstruction (e.g., using distance matrix spectral properties or k-NN graph curvature), (c) exploring problem-size-adaptive normalization that scales with n without introducing instability, and (d) validating whether the slight performance gap is reducible via ensemble heuristics or co-optimization with ACO algorithm parameters. The overarching insight endures: in ACO for TSP, heuristic effectiveness is not about information richness but about providing a clear, symmetric, and stable signal that complements—not competes with—pheromone learning.
