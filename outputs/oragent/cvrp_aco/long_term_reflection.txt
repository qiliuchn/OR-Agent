The latest round of experiments (Experiments #1–#18) has significantly deepened and refined our understanding of effective static heuristic design for the Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem (CVRP) within Ant Colony Optimization (ACO). The core principles established in prior reflection—geometric intuition, multiplicative coherence, empirical feasibility, and disciplined minimalism—have been not only validated but sharpened through rigorous testing, ablation, and attempted innovation.

First, **polar-angle clustering remains unchallenged as the optimal spatial partitioning strategy**, despite sophisticated alternatives like demand-weighted Voronoi tessellation (#15), ML-refined boundaries (#18), or two-pass local search adjustments (#3). These attempts consistently underperformed (scores 7.085, 7.106, 7.076 respectively), confirming that simplicity aligned with CVRP’s radial route structure is paramount. However, **parameter tuning of cluster weights is highly impactful**: boosting same-cluster edges to 2.0 and reducing cross-cluster penalties to 0.5 (#13, #15) yielded scores as low as 7.0347, suggesting that while the clustering *method* should remain simple, its *strength* can be aggressively tuned.

Second, **multiplicative fusion is not merely preferred—it is non-negotiable**. Experiment #10’s catastrophic failure with additive weighting (score ~13.78) and consistent degradation across all linear-combination attempts reaffirm that ACO’s probabilistic construction requires joint desirability signals. Multiplication enforces consensus among complementary heuristics; addition permits compensation that corrupts edge quality assessment.

Third, **empirical feasibility estimation via random route simulation is irreplaceable**, but its implementation details matter critically. Pure random permutations outperform structured hybrids (#1, #5, #9, #17), confirming that **diversity of load states trumps realism of routes**. However, the number of simulations exhibits diminishing returns: 20–50 runs suffice, and increasing beyond 100 (#13) adds computational cost without proportional gains. Moreover, **simulation-based feasibility cannot be replaced by analytical proxies** (#11, #16, #17)—whether via kernel density, learned models, or dynamic programming—as these fail to capture the combinatorial complexity of feasible load trajectories.

Fourth, **the explicit capacity utilization factor (cap_factor) is largely redundant** when a robust empirical feasibility estimator is present. Multiple ablation studies (#4, #8, #14) show that removing cap_factor or applying it conditionally (e.g., only when feasibility < 0.3) yields comparable or slightly better performance (scores 7.0496, 7.0365) than retaining it unconditionally. This suggests that **empirical simulation already encodes sufficient capacity-aware guidance**, and adding explicit cap_factor introduces noise or overemphasis.

Fifth, **scalability-focused approximations of insertion cost consistently degrade performance**. k-NN approximations (#2, #6, #7, #13, #16) all underperform the full O(n²) calculation, even when enhanced with demand weighting or contextual neighborhoods. The insertion cost component relies on global pairwise relationships that local approximations miss, particularly on larger instances (n=50). This confirms that **heuristic quality outweighs computational efficiency in the static precomputation phase**—optimization efforts should focus on vectorized implementations rather than approximation.

Persistent challenges remain acute: (1) **instance-size sensitivity**—parameters tuned on n=20 don’t perfectly generalize to n=50, though aggressive cluster weighting narrows the gap; (2) **computational overhead** of simulation-based feasibility and O(n²) insertion cost limits scalability to n≥100, yet no approximation preserves quality; (3) **numerical stability** in multiplicative fusion can cause underflow, suggesting logarithmic summation may be worth exploring; and (4) the **static-dynamic gap** remains fundamental—no static heuristic can encode real-time remaining capacity or partial route state.

Promising research directions now crystallize with greater precision: (1) **Double down on parameter tuning of proven components**—especially cluster weights and depot demand scaling—rather than architectural changes; (2) **Develop vectorized, GPU-accelerated implementations** of the full insertion cost and simulation loop to preserve quality while improving scalability; (3) **Permanently remove or conditionally apply cap_factor** to reduce redundancy and noise; (4) **Conduct systematic ablation with fixed random seeds** to isolate true component contributions from stochastic variance; and (5) **Accept that the current five-component model (inverse distance, demand efficiency, polar clustering, insertion cost, empirical feasibility) is near-optimal**—future gains will come from refinement, not reinvention.

The overarching lesson is one of **elegant sufficiency**: the best heuristics are not those that simulate the solver, but those that provide clear, complementary, and geometrically coherent signals that the ACO framework can interpret into high-quality solutions. Trust the constructive process—guide it with simplicity, not complexity.
